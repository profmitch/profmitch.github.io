The Typical Chem 3A
Lecture and Lab Experience

I hope to make an effort to relate to you, the student, and what you have going on in your life, since the era of my formative years were very different than your formative years (see box below)

If I were a student in my own course, this would be my typical experience of a day of lecture and lab.

Before Lecture and Lab

Outside of the course

Are you well-rested? Getting at least 6-7 hours of UNINTERRUPTED sleep has been established by science as what it necessary for health of the body and especially the brain.

Are you well hydrated? This is Fresno in the summer, with its triple digits temperature of dry air. It will suck the water out of you. Understand what being well-hydrated is. Most of your fluid intake should be just plain water and not just coffee and tea.

Are you well nourished? And I don't just mean fast food calories and ultraprocessed garbage with high fructose corn syrup in amounts to gag you. Make sure that you learn about your nutritional needs and how promotes your succcess in academics. Try the Mediterranean diet: it was my healthiest two decades when I lived over there.

For the course

I did my reading, then the pre-lecture quiz. I'm good. The instructor provided the PowerPoint for the lecture and the lab intro, and I gave it a good look, so I am happy.

Okay, that's nice. But keep in mind other things one does for lab prep. On the day of your lab, have you completed the reading for the experiment in your printed manual? Have you completed the Pre-Lab quiz? Does today's lab require safety equipment: goggles and coat? Most labs do. Did you bring them? Do I have my printed lab manual that I should bring to lab?

Lecture

What will you do and what will you need in lecture? .

Note-taking: brought my laptop/tablet, or my notebook and pen.
Some students dont take notes at all. Some practically enter every word I say in writing. This is all about your preference. The most successful students will WRITE DOWN a summary of what they are either reading or listening to, since the act of writing it is part of active and not passive learning. Do what promotes your routine in learning.

My device connecting to internet: it will be used to record attendance with code given in lecture.

Techniques for staying awake during lecture: Stimulant in a thermos bottle or other liquid stimulant. Getting up and going outside a few minutes to breathe air or have a smoke.
The instructor is also getting better at including slides that ask for or demand student participation, like answering an unscored quiz-like question. The instructor in some lectures will "take a break" and talk about something not related to chemistry, such as "what's your favorite getaway?" or other personal questions or statements.

Lab

Phone/laptop/tablet. You will need one for doing the online form work in lab. The College will provide you a laptop to use if you need it, but I recommend you try to bring and use your own smart device. If you will use a College laptop during lab, you need to inform me at the very start of lab so I can distrbute one to those who need one.

Find your (regular) lab partner at start of session. If your partner is a no-show, the instructor will set you up with another if you don't find one yourself. You are NOT to make a group of three unless the instructor has okayed it. We want to keep teams to two, if possible.

Short intro talk by instructor. The instructor will take hopefully 10 or less minutes to intro the lab and talk about things to look for, or to draw your attention to parts of the procedures that need that special understanding.

A lab has two phases.
The first phase is measurements and data gathering. This is the part that can ONLY be done in the lab, so if any mistakes, the procedure must be repeated until it's done right. You not only must record measured quantities and data in your printed lab manual, but also use the online form, which exists actually to validate and verify your measurements to make sure you followed instructions correctly. If the online form or your instructor sees mistakes or unexpected recordings in your data form here, you get the information so you can go back to an instrument or equipment to get it right.

The second phase is about calculations and answering questions for the report. This is stuff you can do in lab or after lab outside of class. You can make mistakes here, but you can easily correct them since the correction does not involve going back to an instrument or equipment to make the correction. Here the online form also helps you make sure your calculations are what they should be as well.

While in the lab, you generally focus strictly on the determination of measured quantities using instruments and equipment and save the calculations for later. The only reason a calculation might be necessary before all instrument/equipment use is complete is if the calculated result is required to make instrument or equipment adjustments for continued data gathering.

Clean-up. When the use of instruments and equipment is all done, do lab cleanup before proceeding to spend additional time in the lab doing calculations.

Leaving early? There are many experiments that do not require the full time of the lab session (to 9:35 pm). Teams can leave early and at the same time—meaning no partner can leave the other unless they agree with each other that they are done—if they have completed their data gathering and if they are leaving their lab space that they used in the condition in which they found it. The lab space is not just their chairs and bench at which they sit, but also if they used reagent bottles and community equipment and which they should make clean. Equipment a team uses must go back in its space or in a designated container for "used/dirty equipment" that will be cleaned by staff. No team should leave early without saying they will leave and asking if their use of space is in a proper condition for leaving lab.

You

The demographic I might see are as follows:

  • Age: Mostly Gen Z, with a healthy number of millennials still. I think I see one or two Gen X students but am not sure. Pretty sure I have not seen a boomer (the last birth year of a boomer would be the age of Kamala Harris). In a couple of years, I will see the first Gen alpha in the class (wow!!). The first Gen beta is still a toddler (say hi)
  • Gender: Since coming to FCC (3rd semester), I think I've generally seen two-thirds (apparently) female, and a third (apparently) male. I use "apparently" to indicate I am not sure and that I am affected by my own personal biases and rigid stereotype thinking that has me making rash assumptions about what gender a person is
  • Culture/ethnicity: this generally reflects the population of California, which has a majority Hispanic and bilingual population. My class includes many other groups from the Far East as well
  • Home/work environment: this is actually what I must understand most about a student in their effort to be successful in this chemistry course. Is the student a full-time student? Or does the student work full-time at a job and is taking chemistry in the evening to move themselves on an academic track slowly? Is the student a caregiver to that student's own child(ren) or providing elder care among other responsibilities? What is the financial security of the student: is there paycheck-to-paycheck stress? Is there family support in multiple ways? Purpose of taking Chem 3A: mostly non-science majors pursuing allied heathcare profession degrees/certificates. Many on transfer track to a bachelors
