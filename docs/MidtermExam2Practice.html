In the midterm are six (6) quantitative questions of multiple-choice type that have value greater than the normal two (2) points for this type of question.

  • Three questions have a value of three (3) points each
  • Three questions have a value of four (4) points each

The point values reflect the effort (time, difficulty) of the question relative to the other multiple choice questions.

Scratch paper use. You will use the blank page to show your work for these six questions in order to get the full score of the question. No work for any question will be assumed to be just taking a guess of the answer in selecting the choice. A correct guess will be worth two (2) points only. Wrong choices will still merit partial credit IF work was shown on the scratch paper.

If this system works satisfactorily, future exams may have the point values for these questions set at 4 and/or 5 points.

Type 2: Energy & Specific Heat Capacity ($q = m \cdot c \cdot \Delta T$) (4 Points)

1. Solving for $c$ (Specific Heat Capacity): A $25.0\text{ g}$ sample of iron (Fe) metal cools from $80.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $20.0^\circ\text{C}$, releasing $675\text{ J}$ of heat energy ($q = -675\text{ J}$). What is the specific heat capacity of iron?

a) $ 0.450\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)} $ b) $ 0.338\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)} $ c) $ 2.25\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)} $ d) $ 0.113\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)} $

2. Solving for $m$ (Mass): A piece of aluminum ($c = 0.897\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)}$) absorbs $1345.5\text{ J}$ of heat energy, causing its temperature to rise from $22.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $52.0^\circ\text{C}$. What is the mass of the aluminum sample?

a) $ 25.0\text{ g} $ b) $ 50.0\text{ g} $ c) $ 60.0\text{ g} $ d) $ 75.0\text{ g} $

3. Solving for $q$ (Heat Energy): How much heat energy ($q$) in joules is required to raise the temperature of a $15.0\text{ g}$ sample of copper metal ($c = 0.385\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)}$) from $25.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $75.0^\circ\text{C}$?

a) $ 288.8\text{ J} $ b) $ 433.1\text{ J} $ c) $ 144.4\text{ J} $ d) $ 577.5\text{ J} $

4. Solving for Temperature ($T_{\text{final}}$): A $50.0\text{ g}$ block of lead ($c = 0.128\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)}$) initially at $20.0^\circ\text{C}$ absorbs $192\text{ J}$ of heat energy. What is the final temperature ($T_{\text{final}}$) of the lead block?

a) $ 30.0^\circ\text{C} $ b) $ 50.0^\circ\text{C} $ c) $ 70.0^\circ\text{C} $ d) $ 80.0^\circ\text{C} $

5. Involving Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$): A $100.0\text{ g}$ sample of liquid water ($c = 4.184\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)}$) at $25.0^\circ\text{C}$ absorbs $10,460\text{ J}$ of heat. What is the final temperature of the water?

a) $ 30.0^\circ\text{C} $ b) $ 45.0^\circ\text{C} $ c) $ 50.0^\circ\text{C} $ d) $ 75.0^\circ\text{C} $

6. Solving for $q$ (Heat Released): A $40.0\text{ g}$ sample of gold ($c = 0.129\text{ J/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)}$) cools from $90.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $30.0^\circ\text{C}$. How much heat energy in joules was released by the gold?

a) $ -309.6\text{ J} $ b) $ -464.4\text{ J} $ c) $ -154.8\text{ J} $ d) $ -619.2\text{ J} $
