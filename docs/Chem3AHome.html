Welcome to Chemistry 3A

In this course you will learn fundamentals about the elements and the atoms which make them up, and about compounds that are made when elements combine with each other to form them, and about how to make use of them to make your life better and interesting.

Communication & Teamwork

A Communication Plan is in the syllabus too. One of the immediate things I learned when I was working in the IT industry for a short period was that in (software development) project management. one of the first things the project manager had to establish was a communications plan. Yeah, in writing. While it is a good thing to have for specifying communication, it is also a CYA document so that no one on the team could try to find the excuse, "But I wasn't told/was aware that we had [or did not have] to do this." You know what I mean.

So please understand that the most successful students are those who have instructors who regularly track their work and who interact with the instructor to make sure they are understanding what is being taught.

Messaging (Canvas Inbox, email) is the basic way but typically not enough.

Office hours* help a lot of students. Part-time faculty do not have an office and they are allowed limited hours during the semester (full-time faculty are obligated a couple of hours each semester).

How You Will Work As a Student Each Week

Lectures will Tuesdays and Thursdays at 5 pm in the lecture room on the first floor of New Science. There will be approximately 60 students in the room learning the concepts of chemistry related to this course.

Laboratories divide the students into two sections of approximately 30 students each. One section will meet after lecture on Tuesdays (the other half of the class gets to go home 😁), the other half on Thursdays. We go upstairs to the 3rd floor of New Science, room 339.

Modules -- it is (supposed to be) customary for teachers to organize the course content and work assignment in a modular way. Such as "Lecture Set 1", "Lecture Set 2", etc. Organization can also be time-based: "Week 1", "Week 2". Whatever makes sense generally.

This course is organized by weeks. Week 1, Week 2, etc.

Each week, you will get homework assignments, have lab experiments, do quizzes that test whether you have prepared for lectures and for laboratories before each session.

Now the lectures themselves may carryover from one session to another, but generally you should get the content you need to do the work.

I will have much more to say about how you should approach the work you are assigned. 

Work outside of the time you are in lecture and laboratory

In addition to the time you spend in lecture and laboratory, there is reading to be done and lecture notes to be reviewed. A quiz before each lecture and a quiz before each laboratory session is done to assess your learning of the material and to ensure you are keeping up. There is homework that looks back (reviews) what has been covered in the course and homework that ensures you are keeping pace. Question banks are ready to use that help you do practice exams and which will also be on the exam.

How Student Work Is Assessed

The syllabus has detail on the type of assessment (homework, quizzes on lecture and lab reading, exams) you will be given and the percentage of that type of work going into determining a course grade. The evaluation also includes some "rules" such as "dropping the lowest or three lowest scores" in evaluating a grade. There will likely be a few opportunities for extra credit: this is often a strong incentive to learning aside for helping to boost points for students who fell short of an assignment for whatever reason.

Take Note of Rules On Laboratory Attendance and Work

Read the Syllabus very carefully on your participation and expected effort and achievement for doing the laboratory part of the course. There are strict expectations on preparation, especially for safety related matters, and for doing your work in a timely matter and the submission of reports. The wearing of approved safety googles and a protective lab coat of the correct length is mandatory.
