HOW-TO: Creating PDF Documents for Upload of Assignments to MyOpenMath & Canvas App

CamScanner

This is mentioned right at the top of this document after a student in the Spring 2026 semester mentioned just how useful and convenient this mobile app is.

It is available in the Android (Play Store) and Apple (app) Store. So start the download and install.

Capture Your Report Pages

  1. Open CamScanner and tap the Camera icon.
  2. Select Batch (or Multiple) mode to scan several pages in one session.
  3. Lay your report flat in a well-lit area with good contrast against the table background.
  4. Hold your phone steady directly above each page and capture all required pages in order.

Adjust and Enhance

  • Check the automatic crop borders for each page and adjust the corners to trim out the desk or background.
  • Select a filter that keeps text and handwritten numbers clear and legible without washing out pencil marks (e.g., Magic Color or B&W / Single Color).

Format and Export as PDF

  1. Tap the title at the top to rename the document (e.g., `Lab1_DataReport_YourName`).
  2. Tap the PDF button at the top or bottom of the screen to assemble all pages into a single document.
  3. Tap Share and select Save to Local / Save to Files (or send the PDF to your email/cloud drive).

Upload to MyOpenMath (MOM)

  1. Open your MyOpenMath assessment in your browser on your phone or computer.
  2. Go to the file submission question, click Choose File (or Browse), and select your saved PDF.
  3. Confirm all pages are attached and submit the assessment.

Imaging and Upload The Images For Work Submission

You have printed matter/pages and you want to image them and upload them to the (Canvas) app. So what is recommended? What are your options? What should you do?

Imaging

Multifunction Office Printer/Scanner

If you have access to that, then use it. It is your best bet. Just open the coil-bound lab manual to the page to be scanned or take the loose leaf page, and put it on the flatbed, glass document table (lift up the feeder lid; don't use the feeder), and scan all the pages, collect them into a single PDF.

The scanner should be set to standard color document, with 300 dpi resolution

Phone Imaging

This is where a lot can go wrong and bad imaging is the result.

You may need to experiment the first time with how to do the imaging and then after that the same method can be followed each time

Lighting. If you use flash, no flash (room in daylight), or a lamp placed behind the phone taking the image, it is best to try all those conditions and then decide which gives the best result. Once you know the conditions, you do not have to experiment again.

Perpendicular placing. Make sure the camera is above the page at a right (90°) angle. Holding the camera at any other angle (like 45°) will give awful results.

Framing. Determine the proper distance of the camera from the page. If it is better to be a foot or two away, why take it from 15 or 30 feet away. By having the page take up nearly all the camera view, the picture resolution for the page is superior.

Preparing the Submission File

If you use the multifunction office printer/scanner, more than likely you have the PDF that looks good and is ready. You are ready to submit.

If the imaging was done with the result that separate image files representing each page are stored in the imaging device (usually smartphone), then it will be necessary to use software that gathers all the image files into a single PDF file so that can be submitted in upload

If the device has an app that creates the bundling PDF file while it is taking multiple images, then that too is an approach, so long as each image shows up well and submitted for your approval, or bad images can be (later) extracted and deleted.

Imaging on phone, processing on desktop/laptop/tablet. Another way of preparing the submission is to use the phone just to do the imaging, and then transfer the image files to the larger device. That transfer can be done wirelessly (Bluetooth, WiFi) using different tools and apps, so that is not difficult.

Confirming the Readiness of The Submission

You would not submit a resume online with a job application without checking many times as to how good the resume looks, right? So why send someone images of your lab report pages that are basically unreadable (blurred, to dark to read/no contrast)? You're not being asked for a resume, true, but the instructor can not grade a lab report that is unreadable. Go over what you are uploading quickly and confirm it's ready.k

Tools & Technologies for Imaging And File Preparation

Google Drive's built-in Scanner: Open the Google Drive app, tap the + (plus) button, and select Scan. You can take a photo of the first page, then tap the + button again to add the next one. Once all pages are captured, tap Done, and the app will process the images and save them as a single PDF.

Adobe Scan: This is a powerful, free app that automatically detects the edges of a document, straightens it, and enhances the text to make it clear. You can capture multiple images and they are automatically combined into one PDF.

Microsoft Lens: Similar to Adobe Scan, this app from Microsoft is excellent for scanning documents and whiteboards. You can take multiple photos and export them as a single PDF.

From iPhone

Students can use a variety of free apps and built-in features on their iPhones to produce a single multi-page PDF from a set of images. The easiest methods are to use either the Photos app or the Files app with its built-in scanner.

Using the Photos App

This is one of the simplest methods if the images are already in your photo library.

  1. Select Images: Open the Photos app and tap Select in the top right corner. Then, tap on each image you want to include in the PDF, making sure to select them in the desired order.

  2. Use the Print Option: Tap the Share button (the box with an arrow pointing up) in the bottom left corner. Scroll down in the options and tap Print.

  3. Save as PDF: Use a two-finger pinch-out gesture on the first page of the print preview to expand it into a full-screen view. You can also press and hold on the thumbnail preview to expand it.

  4. Save the PDF: Tap the Share button again in the top right corner. A new menu will pop up with options to save or share the PDF. Choose Save to Files to save the document to a folder on your iPhone or iCloud.

Using the Files App

This method is great for creating a multi-page PDF from scratch by scanning physical documents.

  1. Open the Files App: Open the Files app and navigate to the folder where you want to save the PDF.

  2. Start a New Scan: Tap the three-dot menu (...) in the top right corner. Select Scan Documents.

  3. Capture Images: Your camera will open. Position the phone over the first page, and the scanner will automatically detect the edges and take a picture. You can also manually take the picture by tapping the shutter button.

  4. Add More Pages: Keep taking pictures of all the pages you need. The iPhone will automatically combine them. Tap Save when you are finished. The app will process the images, and the new multi-page PDF will appear in the folder you chose.

Free PDF SOFTWARE Utilities

This software can be used to take images or PDF files representing images (single pages) and bundling them into a multi-page PDF file.

There are ONLINE PDF creation tools where you upload/drag-and-drop your content to a remote system and it will produce the output for you to download to use. Most of the time, these are OK. Some require you to create an account or tell you they will send you a link to a download to your email address: these might possibly invite annoying spam to your account (I always use an email account/address that I consider to be used for potentially spamming emailers, one you might throw away once a year for such things). Another consideration is privacy: it's okay to use for student homework, but no one should ever use these tools for very sensitive private information. These are things you already know.  These are the online tools (the following list is verbatim from Gemini):

  • Adobe Acrobat Online: Adobe offers a free online tool for merging files. You can upload images and PDFs, reorder them, and combine them into one file. It's a reliable choice from the company that created the PDF format
  • Smallpdf: This is a very popular and user-friendly online suite. It has a "Merge PDF" tool that lets you upload images (JPG, PNG, etc.) and PDF files, rearrange their order with drag-and-drop, and then combine them into a single PDF. They offer a limited number of free uses per day.
  • PDF24 Tools: This is another excellent, completely free online suite. Its "Merge PDF" tool works similarly to Smallpdf. It's a great option because it's completely free and doesn't have a usage limit.
  • iLovePDF: A very comprehensive and popular online service with a user-friendly interface for merging, splitting, and converting PDFs.

Again be cautious whenever you upload any content to these systems/utilities that especially is not student homework.

Desktop PC/Mac software is of course the kind you install on your computer. Here's what Gemini has to say:

  • PDFsam Basic: This is a free and open-source desktop application available for Windows, Mac, and Linux. Its "Merge" feature is powerful, allowing you to combine PDF files, select specific pages, and even add bookmarks. While it's primarily for PDFs, you can use a separate free tool to first convert your images to individual PDFs and then merge them with PDFsam.

  • PDFCreator: Another free and popular tool for Windows. It installs as a virtual printer, allowing you to "print" images and other documents directly into a single PDF file. It also has a built-in feature to combine multiple files.

I have also used software called PDFgear and there is something called PDF-XChange Editor that I have not used much

And here are some more tips from Gemini on using this stuff, some of which I have stated above:

  • Check File Limits: Some online tools have limits on the number of files you can upload at once or the total file size.

  • Privacy: Be mindful that you are uploading your files to a server when using online tools. Most reputable services, like the ones listed above, state that they delete your files after a short period.

  • Image to PDF First: If you're using a tool that only merges PDFs (like some features of PDFsam), you'll need to first convert your images into single-page PDF files. Most online tools have a "JPG to PDF" or "PNG to PDF" converter for this purpose.
