61. The isotope $\text{Lead}-210$ ($^{210}_{82}\text{Pb}$) undergoes beta ($\beta$) decay. What is the resulting daughter nucleus?
a) $^{210}_{81}\text{Tl}$ (Thallium)
b) $^{206}_{80}\text{Hg}$ (Mercury)
*c) $^{210}_{83}\text{Bi}$ (Bismuth)
d) $^{211}_{82}\text{Pb}$ (Lead)
... $^{210}_{83}\text{Bi}$ (Bismuth)**. In $\beta$ decay, a neutron converts to a proton and an electron. The mass number (A) remains the same, but the atomic number (Z) increases by one ($82 + 1 = 83$).

62. A medical dose contains $80.0\text{ mg}$ of the radioisotope $\text{Technetium}-99\text{m}$ ($^{99\text{m}}_{43}\text{Tc}$), which has a half-life of $6.0$ hours. What mass of the radioisotope will remain after $24.0$ hours?
a) $20.0\text{ mg}$
b) $10.0\text{ mg}$
*c) $5.00\text{ mg}$
d) $2.50\text{ mg}$
... $5.00\text{ mg}$**. The number of half-lives ($n$) is $24.0\text{ hr} / 6.0\text{ hr} = 4$. The remaining mass is $80.0\text{ mg} \times (1/2)^4 = 5.00\text{ mg}$.

63. Which of the following ranks the three main types of radiation in order of **increasing penetrating power** (from least penetrating to most penetrating)?
a) Gamma ($\gamma$), Beta ($\beta$), Alpha ($\alpha$)
b) Alpha ($\alpha$), Gamma ($\gamma$), Beta ($\beta$)
c) Beta ($\beta$), Alpha ($\alpha$), Gamma ($\gamma$)
*d) Alpha ($\alpha$), Beta ($\beta$), Gamma ($\gamma$)
e) Beta ($\beta$), Gamma ($\gamma$), Alpha ($\alpha$)
... Alpha ($\alpha$), Beta ($\beta$), Gamma ($\gamma$)**. Alpha particles, being massive and charged, are the least penetrating. Gamma rays are highly energetic photons and are the most penetrating.

64. In a nuclear fission reactor using $\text{U}-235$ as fuel, what is the primary function of the **control rods**?
a) To increase the energy of the neutrons to speed up the fission reaction.
b) To serve as the fuel source that sustains the chain reaction.
*c) To absorb excess **neutrons** to prevent an out-of-control fission event (nuclear explosion).
d) To boil water and convert it into steam for the turbines.

65. To absorb excess neutrons to prevent an out-of-control fission event (nuclear explosion)**. Control rods contain materials (like cadmium or boron) that are excellent neutron absorbers, thereby regulating the rate of the chain reaction.
    How is electrical power ultimately generated in a conventional nuclear power plant?
a) Neutrons from the fission reaction are directly converted into an electric current.
b) The potential energy of the control rods is converted into electrical energy.
*c) The heat from the nuclear disintegration is used to **boil water**, creating steam that turns a turbine connected to a generator.
d) Gamma rays are captured and immediately converted into direct current (DC) electricity.
... The heat from the nuclear disintegration is used to boil water, creating steam that turns a turbine connected to a generator**. The nuclear reaction's heat is used to generate steam, which drives a conventional electric turbine.

66. Ionizing radiation can damage biomolecules like $\text{DNA}$ in living organisms. Which process represents the **indirect** damage mechanism of this radiation?
a) The high-energy radiation particle directly colliding with and breaking the $\text{DNA}$ backbone.
*b) The radiation knocking an electron off a nearby water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) molecule, creating highly reactive, damaging chemical species.
c) The heat generated by the radiation increasing the temperature of the tissue.
d) Neutrons being absorbed by the $\text{DNA}$ molecule, making it radioactive.
... The radiation knocking an electron off a nearby water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) molecule, creating highly reactive, damaging chemical species**. Indirect damage occurs when radiation ionizes surrounding water, which then chemically attacks the $\text{DNA}$.

67. A scientist measures a sample and finds it has absorbed $0.05\text{ J}$ of radiation energy per kilogram of tissue mass. What is this dose in terms of **rad**?
a) $50\text{ rad}$
*b) $5\text{ rad}$
c) $0.05\text{ rad}$
d) $0.5\text{ rad}$
... $5\text{ rad}$**. The dose is $0.05\text{ J/kg}$, which equals $0.05\text{ Gy}$ (Gray). Since $1\text{ rad} = 0.01\text{ Gy}$, the dose is $0.05\text{ Gy} / 0.01\text{ Gy/rad} = 5\text{ rad}$.

68. The primary cause of an unstable atomic nucleus undergoing radioactive decay is related to which fundamental concept?
a) The $\text{pH}$ of the surrounding solution.
*b) The lack of sufficient **nuclear binding energy** (the 'glue') to hold the protons and neutrons together in the correct ratio.
c) The loss of electrons from the outer valence shell.
d) The surrounding temperature, which lowers the activation energy for decay.
... The lack of sufficient nuclear binding energy (the 'glue') to hold the protons and neutrons together in the correct ratio**. Unstable nuclei decay because the ratio of protons to neutrons is incorrect, resulting in insufficient binding energy.

69. What is the principle behind using **radiometric dating** to determine the age of a fossil or ancient artifact?
a) Measuring the total number of protons in the sample and comparing it to modern standards.
b) Calculating the time it takes for an atom to fuse with another atom.
*c) Determining the ratio of a parent radioisotope to its stable daughter product, using the radioisotope's known **half-life**.
d) Exposing the sample to $\text{X-rays}$ to observe how the energy affects the photographic film.
... Determining the ratio of a parent radioisotope to its stable daughter product, using the radioisotope's known half-life**. The constant rate of decay (half-life) allows scientists to calculate the time since the radioactive material was fixed within the sample.

70. Which statement correctly describes a key difference between **nuclear fission** (used in current power plants) and **nuclear fusion** (the process in the sun)?
a) Fission involves light nuclei combining, while fusion involves heavy nuclei splitting.
b) Fission is controlled by magnetic fields, while fusion uses mechanical control rods.
*c) Fusion requires incredibly high temperatures (millions of degrees), whereas fission operates at or near room temperature.
d) Fission produces less radioactive waste than fusion.
e) Fusion is currently used globally for commercial electricity generation, while fission remains experimental.
... Fusion requires incredibly high temperatures (millions of degrees), whereas fission operates at or near room temperature**. Fusion requires millions of degrees Celsius to overcome the electrostatic repulsion of the positive nuclei.

77. A sample of a radioisotope has a half-life of 10 years. If the initial mass is 80 g, how much remains after 30 years?

78. Convert 2.0 Gy of radiation dose into rad

79. If a tissue absorbs 0.5 Gy of radiation with RBE = 2, what is the dose in sieverts (Sv)?
a) 0.25 Sv  
b) 0.5 Sv  
*c) 1.0 Sv  
d) 2.0 Sv  
... Sv = RBE \times Gy = 2 \times 0.5 = 1.0 Sv.

80. Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5730 years. A fossil bone contains 25% of its original C-14. Approximately how old is the fossil?
a) 5730 years  
*b) 11,460 years  
c) 17,190 years  
d) 22,920 years  
... 25% = 2 half-lives → 2 \times 5730 = 11,460 years.
